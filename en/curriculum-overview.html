Curriculum overview

Audience

This curriculum is for staff of Canadian humanitarian and development organizations. Participants may work in program, operations, technical, or leadership roles. Some will be new to AI. Others will already use approved tools at work.

No paid AI account is required. No real beneficiary, staff, donor, or partner records are used in exercises.

Prerequisites

  • Basic familiarity with your organization’s data and communications policies
  • Ability to join a 90-minute online or in-person session
  • Optional: read the one-page AI use decision guide before Session 1

Learning pathway

Session Level Focus Duration
Session 1 Level 1 Responsible AI foundations 90 minutes
Session 2 Level 2 Verified work by role 90 minutes
Session 3 Level 3 Governance and adoption 90 minutes
Follow-up clinic All levels Workplace application 60 minutes

Sessions are designed to be delivered once per month. Optional ten-minute practice between sessions uses only fictional or approved low-risk tasks.

Learning outcomes

By the end of the series, participants will be able to:

  1. Screen a task using a seven-step risk screen and record a green, amber, or red decision with a named owner.
  2. Compare AI-supported drafts to authorized sources and mark unsupported claims, contradictions, and harmful omissions.
  3. Improve a prompt by naming the task, audience, permitted sources, output format, and a rule for missing facts.
  4. Protect data by refusing to enter personal or sensitive operational information into unapproved tools.
  5. Name human control points including who approves, can override, and provides remedy.
  6. Draft a bounded pilot charter with a non-AI baseline, consultation requirements, success measures, and a safe exit.

Canadian legal and accessibility context

This kit aligns with federal automated decision-making guidance and OPC generative AI principles. Organizations must also apply PIPEDA (and Quebec Law 25 where relevant), provincial privacy laws, and accessibility standards such as the Accessible Canada Act and AODA (Ontario) to their own materials and platforms. References point to primary sources; this kit is not legal advice.

Indigenous data sovereignty and partner-led governance are named in Session 3. Classroom exercises are not a substitute for nation-specific or community-led consultation.

Adapting for 15 to 40 participants

For skills assessment during case practice, facilitators should aim for no more than 8 participants per breakout group so each person can complete the worksheet. At 31 to 40 attendance, use parallel breakouts with co-facilitators or accept worksheet submission within 48 hours for rubric scoring.

Group sizing

Attendance Suggested groups Notes
15 to 20 3 to 4 groups of 4 to 5 Start in pairs within groups
21 to 30 4 to 5 groups of 5 to 6 Rotate reader, recorder, risk challenger, source checker
31 to 40 5 groups of 6 to 8 Use pairs for breakout tasks; plenary debrief; consider co-facilitator

Online delivery

  • Share worksheets as editable files or no-login web forms before the session
  • Use typed chat as an alternative to spoken breakout reports
  • Enable captions; confirm who manages platform accessibility settings at inception
  • Do not record breakouts or use AI meeting assistants on participant discussions

In-person delivery

  • Provide printed worksheets for participants who prefer paper
  • Post slide content on a shared drive at least five business days before each session
  • Allow a no-camera equivalent: written participation routes online

Low-bandwidth adaptations

  • Distribute case materials as plain text before the session
  • Synchronous: phone dial-in plus typed chat; facilitator reads contributions aloud (see technology checklist)
  • Asynchronous fallback when live join is impossible: worksheet within 48 hours and debrief window
  • Offer a text-only participation route without breakout rooms
  • See facilitation tips in each session folder for more detail

Materials included

Each session folder contains:

  • Facilitator run sheet (minute by minute)
  • Learning objectives
  • Slide deck (markdown source, HTML, PDF)
  • Speaker notes
  • Facilitation tips
  • Participant worksheet
  • Answer key and debrief questions

Additional resources: eight synthetic case studies, pre/post assessment, skills rubric, evaluation form, decision guide, data-responsibility checklist, facilitator guide, participant handbook, and references.

Assessment approach

Performance scenarios scored with the four-point skills rubric are the primary pre/post measure. Five self-efficacy items are secondary. An optional 12-item knowledge check supplements reporting. See the assessment folder for scoring guidance and how to read small samples constructively.

Delivery and adaptation resources

  • Facilitator delivery guide, preparation checklist, and sample invitation email
  • Participant handbook and technology and accessibility checklist (captioning plan; synchronous phone-plus-chat low-bandwidth route)
  • Visual aids with diagrams and text equivalents
  • AAP adaptation design note for organizations adapting the kit (no claim of prior community consultation in this repository)
  • Optional Session 2 live-tool lab for org-approved tools with synthetic data only
