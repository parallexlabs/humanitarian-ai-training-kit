# What is generative AI?

- Creates text, images, code, or audio from prompts
- Does **not** guarantee truth
- Can sound confident when wrong
- Examples: drafting, summarizing, translating

*Not the same as every "AI" label on software.*

---

# Prediction and automation

| Type | Does | Humanitarian example |
|------|------|---------------------|
| Prediction | Scores risk from patterns | Flood exposure mapping |
| Automation | Runs fixed rules | Auto-routing forms |
| Generative | Produces new content | Draft report from notes |

All require governance. Generative tools need extra verification.

# Accountability

**Question:** If AI supports a decision, who is accountable?

- The vendor? **No**
- The model? **No**
- A **named person** in your organization? **Yes**

Human approval, override, and remedy are required for consequential uses.

---

# Protection lens

Before the tool, ask:

- Who benefits? Who may be excluded?
- Did affected people and local partners shape the task?
- How can someone challenge or correct the result?

Fundamental Principles: humanity, impartiality, neutrality, independence.

---

# Worked example: the decision

**Task:** Review a draft evacuation SMS. You may **not** send it.

**Source W1 (10:00):** Reception Centre A open. Only Entrance E is step-free. No road route confirmed.

**Draft:** "Road B open. All entrances step-free. Go now."

---

# Worked example: source check

| Statement | W1 | Mark |
|-----------|-----|------|
| Road B open | No route info | Not established |
| All entrances step-free | Only E | Contradicted |
| Go now | No send authority | Not established |

# Seven-step screen (overview)

1. Purpose and alternative
2. People and partners
3. Data
4. Tool and vendor
5. Verification
6. Human control
7. Monitoring and stop

**Decision:** Green / Amber / Red + owner + review date

---

# Group exercise

Apply the seven-step screen
