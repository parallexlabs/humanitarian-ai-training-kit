# Pre/post assessment

**Kirkpatrick level 2 (learning).** Administer before Session 1 and after Session 3. Use different scenario examples at post-test (same constructs). Participant-held codes link responses without names.

**Reference:** Kirkpatrick, J. D., & Kirkpatrick, W. K. (2016). [*Kirkpatrick's Four Levels of Training Evaluation*](https://www.td.org/product/book--kirkpatricks-four-levels-of-training-evaluation/111614). ATD Press.

## Part B: Self-efficacy (secondary measure)

Use a 5-point scale: 1 = not at all confident, 5 = very confident.

1. I can decide when AI is appropriate for a work task.
2. I can check an AI output against a trusted source.
3. I can protect sensitive data when using digital tools.
4. I can explain my AI use decision to a colleague or manager.
5. I can identify when to escalate to privacy, protection, or IT.

Report mean and distribution. Treat as **secondary** to rubric scores. A rise of 0.5 points or more may indicate increased confidence, but it is not proof of workplace behaviour change.

## Part C: Knowledge items (supplementary, 12 items)

Optional multiple-choice check. Score 1 point per correct item. Use different stems at post-test.

### Sample pre-test items

1. **Data:** A colleague wants to paste beneficiary names into a free online chatbot to draft a letter. What is the best first response?
   - A) Stop and check whether the tool is approved for personal data ✓
   - B) Help them write a better prompt
   - C) Anonymize only the surnames
   - D) Proceed if the chat is deleted afterward

2. **Verification:** An AI summary says "78% of households lack clean water." What should you do before using this number?
   - A) Round it to 80% for clarity
   - B) Trust it if it sounds reasonable
   - C) Find the original source and check date ✓
   - D) Add a footnote saying "AI generated"

3. **Translation:** A French SMS alert says roads are open when the English source says they are closed. What is the highest-priority risk?
   - A) Spelling errors
   - B) Wrong tone
   - C) Character limit
   - D) Dropped or reversed negation affecting safety ✓

4. **Human control:** Who is accountable when AI supports a decision about aid eligibility?
   - A) The AI vendor
   - B) A named organizational decision-maker ✓
   - C) The person who typed the prompt
   - D) No one if the AI confidence score is high

5. **Tool:** Your organization has not approved any generative AI tools. A staff member uses one for internal meeting notes. Best classification?
   - A) Amber ✓
   - B) Green
   - C) Red
   - D) Green if notes are deleted

6. **Protection:** A chatbot tells a beneficiary where to collect cash assistance. The location is wrong. Primary harm?
   - A) Embarrassment
   - B) Extra travel cost only
   - C) Brand damage
   - D) Exclusion or safety risk from acting on false information ✓

7. **Purpose:** AI is proposed to draft a donor report from last year's files. First question?
   - A) Which model is best?
   - B) Can we finish by Friday?
   - C) What is the manual alternative and is AI necessary? ✓
   - D) Who has the longest prompt guide?

8. **Governance:** A pilot AI tool will classify incoming calls. Minimum governance step?
   - A) Buy the cheapest licence
   - B) Define success, failure threshold, human override, and exit plan ✓
   - C) Train all staff on prompting
   - D) Publish a press release

9. **Sharing:** A partner asks you to upload a shared spreadsheet to an AI tool they provide. You should:
   - A) Upload if the partner insists
   - B) Upload only rows without names
   - C) Refuse all partner tools
   - D) Check your organization's approval and data-sharing agreement ✓

10. **Bias:** An AI-generated needs summary mentions only urban areas. What principle applies?
    - A) Harmful omission and exclusion ✓
    - B) Efficiency
    - C) Translation quality
    - D) Cost recovery

11. **Monitoring:** When should you stop using an approved AI tool?
    - A) Never, if it was approved once
    - B) Only when the licence expires
    - C) When it fails a set threshold, terms change, or harm is reported ✓
    - D) When a newer model launches

12. **Accountability:** Classroom role play about consultation with affected people is:
    - A) A substitute for real consultation
    - B) Legally sufficient
    - C) Practice only; it does not replace consultation with affected people ✓
    - D) The same as a community meeting

## Scoring guide

| Section | Scoring | Report | Role |
|---------|---------|--------|------|
| Performance scenarios | 0 to 4 per scenario | Mean pre and post; paired gain | **Primary** |
| Self-efficacy | 1 to 5 per item | Mean per item pre and post | Secondary |
| Knowledge (optional) | 0 to 12 | Mean, median, % at 10+ | Supplementary |

## How to read results with small samples

- **Small n (15 to 40):** Aggregates are informative for your cohort; subgroup analysis is not reliable. Report n for every metric.
- **Paired vs unpaired:** Report how many participants completed both pre and post scenario sets. Do not impute missing scores.
- **No causal claims:** Pre/post without a control group shows association after training, not proof the training caused change.
- **Confidence vs performance:** Self-efficacy can rise without improved rubric scores. Lead reports with scenario performance, not confidence alone.
- **Workplace transfer:** Follow-up self-report is optional context, not verified impact.
- **Use results constructively:** Low rubric scores after training signal where to reinforce (verification, human control, or governance), not failure of participants.

## Privacy

Store anonymous codes only. Do not collect names on the same form as scenario answers unless your organization has a separate consent process.
