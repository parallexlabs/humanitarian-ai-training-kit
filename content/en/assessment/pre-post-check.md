# Pre/post knowledge and confidence check

**Administer before Session 1 and after Session 3.** Use different example items at post-test (same constructs). Participant-held codes link responses without names.

## Part A: Knowledge items (12)

Score each item 1 point for correct, 0 for incorrect. Target proficiency: 10/12 or higher at post-test (organizational choice).

### Sample pre-test items

1. **Data:** A colleague wants to paste beneficiary names into a free online chatbot to draft a letter. What is the best first response?
   - A) Help them write a better prompt
   - B) Stop and check whether the tool is approved for personal data ✓
   - C) Anonymize only the surnames
   - D) Proceed if the chat is deleted afterward

2. **Verification:** An AI summary says "78% of households lack clean water." What should you do before using this number?
   - A) Round it to 80% for clarity
   - B) Find the original source and check date ✓
   - C) Trust it if it sounds reasonable
   - D) Add a footnote saying "AI generated"

3. **Translation:** A French SMS alert says roads are open when the English source says they are closed. What is the highest-priority risk?
   - A) Spelling errors
   - B) Wrong tone
   - C) Dropped or reversed negation affecting safety ✓
   - D) Character limit

4. **Human control:** Who is accountable when AI supports a decision about aid eligibility?
   - A) The AI vendor
   - B) The person who typed the prompt
   - C) A named organizational decision-maker ✓
   - D) No one if the AI confidence score is high

5. **Tool:** Your organization has not approved any generative AI tools. A staff member uses one for internal meeting notes. Best classification?
   - A) Green
   - B) Amber ✓
   - C) Red
   - D) Green if notes are deleted

6. **Protection:** A chatbot tells a beneficiary where to collect cash assistance. The location is wrong. Primary harm?
   - A) Embarrassment
   - B) Exclusion or safety risk from acting on false information ✓
   - C) Extra travel cost only
   - D) Brand damage

7. **Purpose:** AI is proposed to draft a donor report from last year's files. First question?
   - A) Which model is best?
   - B) What is the manual alternative and is AI necessary? ✓
   - C) Can we finish by Friday?
   - D) Who has the longest prompt guide?

8. **Governance:** A pilot AI tool will classify incoming calls. Minimum governance step?
   - A) Buy the cheapest licence
   - B) Define success, failure threshold, human override, and exit plan ✓
   - C) Train all staff on prompting
   - D) Publish a press release

9. **Sharing:** A partner asks you to upload a shared spreadsheet to an AI tool they provide. You should:
   - A) Upload if the partner insists
   - B) Check your organization's approval and data-sharing agreement ✓
   - C) Upload only rows without names
   - D) Refuse all partner tools

10. **Bias:** An AI-generated needs summary mentions only urban areas. What principle applies?
    - A) Efficiency
    - B) Harmful omission and exclusion ✓
    - C) Translation quality
    - D) Cost recovery

11. **Monitoring:** When should you stop using an approved AI tool?
    - A) Never, if it was approved once
    - B) When it fails a set threshold, terms change, or harm is reported ✓
    - C) Only when the licence expires
    - D) When a newer model launches

12. **Accountability:** Classroom role play about consultation with affected people is:
    - A) A substitute for real consultation ✓
    - B) Legally sufficient
    - C) Required by UNESCO
    - D) The same as a community meeting

### Post-test

Replace examples (e.g., procurement memo, protection note, vendor pitch) while testing the same constructs. Swap distractors. Do not reuse identical stems.

## Part B: Self-efficacy (5 items)

Use a 5-point scale: 1 = not at all confident, 5 = very confident.

1. I can decide when AI is appropriate for a work task.
2. I can check an AI output against a trusted source.
3. I can protect sensitive data when using digital tools.
4. I can explain my AI use decision to a colleague or manager.
5. I can identify when to escalate to privacy, protection, or IT.

Report mean and distribution. A rise of 0.5 points or more may indicate increased confidence, but this is not proof of workplace behaviour change.

## Scoring guide

| Section | Scoring | Report |
|---------|---------|--------|
| Knowledge | 0 to 12 | Mean, median, % at 10+ |
| Self-efficacy | 1 to 5 per item | Mean per item pre and post |

## Honest interpretation

- **Small n:** With 15 to 40 participants, subgroup analysis is unreliable. Report aggregates only.
- **No causal claims:** Pre/post without a control group cannot prove the training caused change. Say "associated with" or "following the series."
- **Missing pairs:** Report unpaired and missing separately. Do not impute scores.
- **Confidence vs skill:** Self-efficacy can rise without improved performance. Use the skills rubric for task evidence.
- **Workplace transfer:** A follow-up survey asks what participants tried at work. Treat responses as self-report, not verified impact.
